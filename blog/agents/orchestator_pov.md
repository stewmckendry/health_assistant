## The Orchestrator Model

By 2030, internet search may be replaced by AI, eliminating the need to choose between different Clinical Decision Support (CDS) models. A “model of models” is likely to emerge, where expert systems are orchestrated with human guidance. This concept is not science fiction—Microsoft has already built and tested such a system. Nori et al. (2025) describe MAI-DxO (Model-Agnostic Diagnostic Orchestrator), which coordinates multiple specialized agents and significantly improves diagnostic accuracy on sequential NEJM-CPC cases compared to single models.

A practical clinical metaphor: imagine a team of AI medical residents supervised by a Chief Resident Orchestrator (also AI). The Orchestrator is not the Attending—that’s your role. The Chief aggregates inputs from multiple Residents (best-evidence, past-practice, SDOH, formulary/SOP, etc.), reports disagreements or alternatives, and supports auditability. Teams are modular by specialty (e.g., psychiatry vs. orthopedics) and can be multidisciplinary. For example, the team could include:

- **SDOH/equity agent:** Finds housing and support resources
- **Finance/formulary agent:** Matches insurance coverage
- **Local SOP/pathway agent:** Helps schedule labs and diagnostics
- **Formulary expert:** Identifies drug interactions and recommends generic substitutions
- (A lawyer—no, that’s probably not a good idea!)

Team members can be rotated based on patient needs. Accountability sits with the Chief Resident, who reports to the Attending. When recommendations differ, the causes of disagreement can be surfaced for review.
